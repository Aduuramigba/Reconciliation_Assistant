OBJECTTIVES 
The aim of this document is to give new users of Corona Cash an overview of the entire 
functionality of Corona Cash. It is designed as a working document; hence a lot of space 
for your notes and comments is available. 
 
More detailed information on every topic can be obtained from the Corona User Guide or 
the incorporated Online Help system of Corona Cash. 
 
 
TRAINING AGENDA 
We can distribute the training in nine major areas and total durations of training of 32 hours as 
follows: 
1. General introduction 
1.1. Business background 
1.1.1. What is Reconciliation? 
Reconciliation is the process of making two or more opposing items match with 
each another by identifying and comparing relevant information to check that what 
we expected to happen did happen when we expected it to. Any discrepancies or 
occurrences of items not reconciling or matching should be investigation to 
ascertain the reason why. 
1.1.2. Why do we reconcile? 
There are several reasons behind why we reconcile, these reasons can be grouped 
into: 
• We don’t 
✓ Want to pay more than once 
✓ Want to be overcharged 
✓ Want to go overdrawn 
• We do 
✓ Want to ensure that ‘they’ 
have paid ‘us’  
✓ Catch unauthorized 
transactions 
✓ Monitor progress of a transaction 
✓ Identify potential problems before they escalate 
✓ Reduce risk / financial exposure 
The list of reasons is endless but regardless of the reason the underlying concept 
is the same, we don’t want to lose money, whether that is due to ‘our’ actions or 
‘their’ actions, incurring interest or payment errors. 
We also want to monitor the progress of transactions and identify potential 
problems before they escalate which in turn will reduce any risk and financial 
exposure and unwanted costs. 
 
‘Corona’ product set is based around ‘reconciliation’.

occurred.

1.1.5. What can go wrong? 
There are many reasons why reconciliations can go wrong, it could be that there 
has been: 
Unrecognized Transactions - An unrecognized transaction could lead to a 
fraudulent posting, or transactions being associated with the wrong account, or 
inconsistent details being used between ledgers and statements. 
Wrong Dates - Incorrect dates could result in money being posted too early 
resulting in wastage or too late incurring interest. 
Wrong Amount – Charges would be accrued for the difference between correct 
and incorrect amount 
Didn’t Happen - Individual messages can be lost and therefore are never 
entered into the reconciliation system. Or funds could be released for an account 
that has insufficient funds to cover the transaction incurring relevant costs 
The list is quite endless and exhaustive, wherever there is human interaction there 
is room for error. 
1.1.6. What is reconciliation of Nostro accounts? 
Reconciliation of Nostro accounts usually involves two parties: the account owner 
(Your own bank) and the account holder (your partner/correspondent bank). 
 
The terms account holder and account owner are only correct in connection with 
Nostro accounts. For other types of accounts, e.g. internal accounts, both parties 
may be within the same bank so these terms would not be correct. For reasons of 
simplicity, however, the above terms will be used throughout this training. 
 
All parties involved in the reconciliation system keep records of all the 
transactions carried out on the account. The account holder sends statements to 
the account owner who will compare these statements with his own records. Thus 
he will see whether the records are identical, i.e. whether there is a mistake in 
one of the records, and whether the transactions performed are correct. 
 
With Corona Cash this process can be fully automated. The statements received 
from the account holder and the account owner's records of the internal booking 
system are introduced into Corona Cash. This may be done via interface 
programs. Basically, Corona Cash needs two interfaces: 
• the interface to SWIFT or some other network (their interface), and 
• The interface to the account owner's internal booking system (our interface). 
 
The account information received from the partner bank is called their 
statement, i.e. from the records or the accounting system of the account holding 
bank. 
 
The account information provided by the account owner is called our statement, 
i.e. from the records or the accounting system of the account owner. 
 
Their statements that are not received via SWIFT or some other network may 
be introduced manually via the screen.

1.2.  Basic terms in account reconciliation 
This chapter lists the most important terms used in connection with Corona Cash. 
It is meant to make you familiar with the keywords, which will be used throughout 
Corona Cash and its documentation. 
1.2.1. The statement 
To be able to compare the transactions carried out on the account, the account 
holder has to send statements to the account owner, listing all the bookings made 
on the account, along with the value dates, the amounts involved and references as 
to the origin/destination of the booking. 
 
Each statement is identified by a statement date and a statement number. This data 
is needed to process the information contained in the statement. 
 
Each valid statement contains two statement balances. Corona Cash checks them to 
see if any statement is missing. The opening balance equals the closing balance of 
the previous statement. The closing balance's amount is calculated as follows: 
opening balance plus/minus all items of a statement. Statement balances reflect 
booked funds and not available funds. 
1.2.2.  The item 
Each financial transaction carried out on an account leads to an entry called item 
that contains all necessary information to the transaction made, i.e. date, amount, 
amount sign, and references. Synonyms are booking, entry, transaction… 
Besides the booking date, an item will also contain a value date, i.e. the date 
when the proceeds of a money transaction will become available for use. 
Apart from references, items may be given a transaction type, which is a special 
code enabling the user to assign the booking to a special group of transactions (e.g. 
charges, cheques) thus allowing for special processing of the item. 
Previously transmitted incorrect items are cancelled by reversed items. 
Reversed debit (amount sign 'RD') cancels debit ('D'), reversed credit ('RC') 
cancels credit ('C'). 
1.2.3. Other basic terms 
The account holder is the bank that manages an account for another bank or 
organization and carries out transactions on this account. The account owner is the 
legal owner of the account and the funds stored on the account. 
To keep track of the account holder's bookings to an account, the account 
owner sets up a mirror account in his internal booking system, which will 
contain the same bookings as the original account, however, with a different 
amount sign? 
Choose among three matching classes per criterion and condition: match 
automatically, create proposal or leave items open. The matching class serves 
to instruct the system what to do with items that meet a specified condition. 
Amount differences between the account holders and internal bookings are 
usually caused by charges to be paid to the account holder. 
Investigation cases can be created to find out more information e.g. on an 
open item.

2.3.1. Managing Methods 
Corona Cash allows you to assign different matching methods for matching via 
reference as well as matching via amount. It may, for example, be useful to set 
the more restrictive matching method 'Nostro' for matching via amount, while 
selecting ‘one-sided' for matching via reference if you want to reconcile internal 
accounts. As the matching methods are at present predefined, they may only be 
viewed. They reflect common bank practice. 
 
Corona Cash supports six different matching methods: 
• <ANY>  
• Internal 
• Mixed  
• Nostro 
• One-sided 
• One-sided net (only according references) 
The following paragraphs provide an overview of the different accounts and 
the related matching methods. 
2.3.1.1. Matching method Internal 
With internal accounts, account owner and account holder are both in the same 
bank or financial institution, e.g. two different departments. Both bookings 
needed for the reconciliation of these accounts originate from the same bank. 
For technical reasons, the bookings from one party are referred to as ‘their’ 
bookings. 
The matching methods Internal reference and Internal amount are best suited for 
handling internal accounts. They have been created following common bank 
practice for reconciling internal accounts. 
2.3.1.2.  Matching method Mixed 
On a mixed account the rules of Nostro account matching are used on the their 
side and those of internal account matching on the our side. In case of an amount 
difference, all items on the statement side must be debit items and the sum of 
credit items must be equal to or smaller than the sum of debit items, i.e. only a 
debit amount difference is allowed.

2.3.1.3.  Matching method Nostro 
A Nostro account is an account which one bank or financial institution holds for 
another bank or financial institution. The account holder carries out the 
transactions requested by the account owner and informs him about all the 
transactions by sending statements (their statements) via SWIFT. The account 
owner will compare these statements with the bookings in the internal system, 
which is also introduced into Corona Cash (our statements). The bookings from 
both sides will then be reconciled in Corona Cash. To be able to match items on a 
Nostro account with an amount difference, all items with the origin Their must be 
debit items and the sum of credit items must be equal to or smaller than the sum of 
debit items, i.e. only a debit amount difference is possible. Reversed items are not 
allowed in case of an amount and/or value date difference. 
Matching methods for Nostro accounts have been predefined following 
common bank practice (Nostro reference, Nostro amount). 
2.3.1.4.  Matching method One-sided 
Generally, one-sided accounts are handled like internal accounts. Both bookings 
come from within the same bank or financial institution. With this account type, 
debit items can be matched with credit items on the their side as well as on the 
our side and the amount sign need not be changed to RD or RC. All items will 
be matched - independent of their origin. The only exception from this rule is 
their credit items and our debit items. On one-sided accounts these items cannot 
be matched via amount. 
These rules are reflected in the predefined matching methods One-sided reference 
and One-sided amount. 
2.3.1.5.  Matching method <ANY> 
This method allows matching RC against RD items. 
When selecting <ANY> in the matching method list any combination of sign and 
origin will be accepted in a possible group (built via reference or amount) e.g. 
OC,OD,OrC,OrD, TC,TD,TrC,TrD,. When aggregating, the difference need to 
remain within the tolerances as defined.

• Matched items are items that have been reconciled manually or in a

• Grouped open items are items that have been grouped and are still open. 
• Open items are items that have neither been matched, proposed or grouped. 
• Contradictory items are items that have triggered the creation of 
contradictory groups as they are part of more than one group - they too are 
still open.

• Non-matched items are all items that have not been matched, i.e. all

3.6. Types of statements 
The status of a statement can be correct, incorrect or non-selected. 
Statements assigned to a physical account are correct cash statements. 
Statements that are assigned to a physical account, but do not fit into the sequence 
of the correct statements upon loading, are incorrect cash statements. 
Cash statements that do not fit into the sequence will not become incorrect if you 
select the Gap option of the physical account. In this case, the statement will be 
accepted as correct, but the system will create an additional statement that stands for 
the missing statement between the last correct and the new statement   
Statements that could not be associated with any physical account are non- 
selected.

5.2.1. Some definitions of interest rates 
Eonia: Euro OverNight Index Average is an effective overnight interest rate 
computed as a weighted average of all overnight unsecured lending transactions in the 
interbank market. It has been initiated within the euro area by the contributing panel 
banks. It is one of the two benchmarks for the money and capital markets in the euro 
zone (the other one being Euribor). The banks contributing to Eonia are the same as 
the Panel Banks quoting for Euribor. 
 
Eonia reference rates are calculated by the European Central Bank, based on all 
overnight interbank assets created before the close of RTGS systems at 6pm CET, 
and published through Thomson Reuters every day before 7pm CET. Informative 
historical data can also be found at the Euribor homepage. It could be found under 
the ISIN identifier EU0009659945. 
LIBOR: London Interbank Offered Rate is a daily reference rate based on the 
interest rates at which banks borrow unsecured funds from other banks in the London 
wholesale money market (or interbank lending market). Alternatively, this can be 
seen from the point of view of the banks making the 'offers', as the interest rate at 
which the banks will lend to each other: that is 'offer' money in the form of a loan for 
various time periods (maturities) and in different currencies. 
EURIBOR: Euro Interbank Offered Rate is a daily reference rate based on the 
averaged interest rates at which Eurozone banks offer to lend unsecured funds to 
other banks in the euro wholesale money market (or interbank market). Euribors are 
used as a reference rate for euro-denominated forward rate agreements, short term 
interest rate futures contracts and interest rate swaps, in very much the same way 
as LIBORs are commonly used for Sterling and US dollar-denominated instruments.

7. Value-dated balances 
Corona Cash is able to calculate value-dated balances (VDB). The calculation of 
VDB serves two requirements: it allows to project future balances helping the 
person in charge to determine whether funds need to be moved from or to a 
certain account to on one hand avoid to be short in a certain currency (and 
consequently having to pay interest) and on the other hand to allow to move funds 
to accounts where interest is gained. Furthermore, it offers the possibility to re-
calculate the interest statement which is received periodically (mostly monthly) 
from the account 
Since the calculation of the VDB may have an impact on the performance of 
Corona Cash, the calculation of VDB has to be activated on Cash account level. 
This functionality is also known and sometimes referred to as Position 
keeping management or Treasury function. 
7.1.1 Activating value-dated balances 
1    Right-click on the cash account for which you want to activate the VDB  
and choose Value-dated balances > Activate value-dated balances. 
 
2 
Enter the initial date for this cash account where the VDB should 
be calculated from. 
(You can use a maximum entry date to calculate the VDB based on 
a certain entry date period.) 
3 
In account information you can enter an initial balance, a 
minimum, maximum and base balance. 
4 
Entering an adjustment period is necessary to keep up the performance on 
loading high volumes into Corona Cash accounts with active VDB. 
Within this period the VDB will be recalculated. The period starts on the 
actual date and ends at the value date of the loaded item. So items with a 
value date more than 7 days in the past will not cause the VDB to be 
recalculated. These items will instead be written into an adjustment table 
and calculated on viewing the VDB. For the Corona user there is no 
difference.

8.6. Corona Archive 
Supervisory authorities, strict national regulations of data retention, tax authorities as 
well as business requirements insist on banks being able not only to retain 
transaction data over long periods of time but also to produce it on request. 
The implications of this situation are manifold. On the one hand, an enormous 
volume of data accumulates over the years. Simply keeping data on the production 
database slows down performance, whereas maintaining hundreds – if not thousands 
– of tapes, cartridges, CDs or DVDs may turn out to be an expensive nightmare. On 
the other hand, stored data must be quickly accessible when needed. However, 
finding specific pieces of information dating back several years is again expensive 
and time-consuming. And once the requested piece of information is found, 
associated data, such as investigations and subsequent related transactions, are still to 
be retrieved. 
Corona Archive is delivered with standard *.par files to transfer the data from the 
history to the Corona Archive database. As soon as the retention periods defined for 
the data records you want to archive have elapsed, you can execute these files. As a 
result, the data is no longer available in the history database. However, provided your 
access profile contains access rights to Corona Archive, you can still easily access this 
data. 
The archiving job can be carried out exclusively in batch mode. For details contact 
your system administrator. 
The Archive filter is your main working area in Corona Archive. Here you specify the 
criteria that restrict the results of your search.

9. EURO Functionality 
The Euro functionality is the part of Corona Cash that is taking care of the transition 
of denomination currencies to the common European currency EUR. 
Converting your data relevant to the euro is done per cash account involving all 
associated physical accounts and items. You can determine when you want to 
convert a particular account and synchronize this step with your partner bank. 
The conversion as such involves the following two steps: 
• Preparations 
• Converting cash accounts 
9.1. Preparation 
It is recommended to “clean up” accounts before starting to convert them, i.e. to close 
statement gaps, match as many items as possible, check statements and items that have 
been entered but not accepted and items, accept or delete incorrect statements and non-
selected statements, etc. 
 
Cash statements and items that have been entered manually but have not been 
accepted will be lost after the conversion. 
 
Before you can convert cash accounts to the euro, you need to carry out the 
following preparations: 
• Defining the currency EUR  
• Defining conversion rates  
• Converting existing matching sets  
• Closing existing statement gaps 
 
9.1.1. Adjusting the currencies 
The first step of your preparations is the definition of the currency EUR. 
Before you can move on to other preparatory steps, the currencies have to be 
activated as EUR denominations in the Currencies list. When converting the base 
currency in the Access Control System to euro, the currency EUR is automatically 
adopted as new base currency for the respective access area. 
 
The Currencies list displays the euro-related status of the currencies:

Following the definition of the denominations and the conversion rates you can

Both matching sets exist in parallel after the conversion. It is necessary to keep both 
matching sets in the beginning because it may not be possible to convert all accounts

the same moment to start converting accounts). The original matching set is required 
during the conversion of accounts in order to calculate the amount limits in euro based 
on the amount limits in the old currency.

9.2. Conversion 
9.2.1. Converting cash accounts 
There are two ways to convert existing cash accounts to the new currency:  
• Copying accounts and transferring cash statements and items 
• Switching cash accounts to the euro 
When to use copy & transfer 
With Nostro accounts where you depend on your partner bank's decision on how and 
when they are going to stop sending statements in the old currency, the copy & transfer 
method is recommended. This function allows you to close the account in the old 
currency with an account balance of zero and create a new account in EUR. The 
denomination account is then deactivated and can no longer be edited. All further 
processing is done on the successor account in euro. 
 
When to switch accounts 
The Switch account to euro method is recommended for internal accounts where you 
can determine exactly when and how you want to initiate the transition to the euro. 
The account basically remains the same with all related data converted to the new 
currency EUR. 
 
Audit trail 
Both functions provide an audit trail which enables you to trace converted account 
data to the original records. 
Before switching or transferring accounts you can check which items will be affected 
by the conversion. In the Select cash items dialog, choose the status None matched.
